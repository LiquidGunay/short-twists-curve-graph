# Lean sources for the genus-two curve graph

Audit date 26 November 2026. The target is a statement about isotopy classes of unoriented essential simple closed curves on a specified closed genus-two surface, with an edge between distinct classes admitting disjoint representatives.

## Existing Lean foundations

Mathlib provides graph distance and continuous homotopies of paths. I checked the contents, including comments and declarations, of all 4,326 files under `Mathlib/` ending in `.lean` at the project's pinned v4.8.0 and all 8,736 at commit `348837d3aa976624060d7c35c183fff587c5a24a`. A case-insensitive search allowing whitespace or underscores between terms covered “Dehn twist”, “curve complex”, “curve graph”, “efficient geodesic”, “annular cover”, “annular projection”, “subsurface projection”, “ribbon graph”, “rotation system”, “ambient isotopy” and “essential/simple closed curve”. It returned no matches in either snapshot. This search may miss different terminology or external developments. I found no implementation there that identifies the manuscript's checker with the surface-graph distance. The separate `classification-of-surfaces` project supplies an explicit topological carrier. In its normal form, `p` counts handle blocks and `n` counts boundary blocks. Its theorem `canonicalOrientableRealizationHomeomorph` identifies the geometric realization of the canonical signed polygon with the corresponding quotient. Specializing to `p = 2` and `n = 0` gives a candidate closed genus-two surface for the geometric formalization. I inspected its 132 source modules under the principal `ClassificationOfSurfaces/` directory. I found no theorem there identifying the essential curves or disjointness of the marked diagrams used here. The inspected project pins Lean 4.32.0. Reusing it would require a separate compatible build or a port. ## The geometric obligations

The local development now constructs the marked embedded essential circles and shear on the real-cylinder quotient for the 14-crossing word. It verifies the 196 representative intersections and the integer crossing order. The remaining identification with the canonical closed genus-two surface, minimal position, and the interpretation of the search require separate surface arguments. [Current formal results](README.md) · [Formal example](SurfaceExamples.lean)

For the **one-twist 14-crossing example**, one needs an identification of the signed diagram with marked embedded circles on this surface, agreement of the collar algorithm with the surface twist, and a proof that the distance test captures all short paths of isotopy classes. The latter uses two geometric facts. At two edges, an intersecting pair admits a common disjoint essential curve precisely when it fails to fill. At greater budgets, an efficient geodesic supplies a bounded first neighbor. Birman–Margalit–Menasco prove existence in Theorem 1.1. Proposition 3.7 explicitly bounds the first neighbor's intersections with each segment of the terminal curve cut by the initial curve by the distance minus two. A Lean proof needs to connect those segments and reference arcs to the actual coordinates enumerated by the checker. [Birman–Margalit–Menasco, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.4133#page=3) · [Proposition 3.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.4133#page=24)

The statement for **all powers** of the twist on the twelve-crossing example adds annular projection. Masur–Minsky's §2.4 uses the diameter of the union of the two projection sets. Its equation (2.6) gives \(\lvert n\rvert+2\) for the projected surface curve and its \(n\)-fold twist, assuming a nonzero exponent and a curve crossing the core. The preceding formula gives \(\lvert n\rvert\) for twisting a single arc directly in the covering annulus. I verified both formulas in the 1998 preprint and on page 17 of Masur's author-hosted manuscript listed as November 2000. The publisher page confirms the journal citation. I have not independently compared its typeset page or located an erratum changing (2.6). [1998 preprint, p. 17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9807150#page=17) · [Masur's dated list](https://math.uchicago.edu/~masur/papers.html) · [Author's 2000 PostScript](https://math.uchicago.edu/~masur/mm2.ps) · [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1007/PL00001643)

Proving the **sharp 14-crossing threshold** also requires showing that every distance-four seed with 12 or 13 crossings admits at least one of the enumerated cutting, triangulation and gluing descriptions. The equivalences used in the census must preserve twisting distance. A proof about the list of generated codes alone leaves this coverage theorem to supply. [Paper, §7](../paper/short-twists.pdf#page=7)
